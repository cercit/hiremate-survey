## Hypotheses this raises (to test in real interviews)
1. **The gap isn't questions, it's being challenged.** YouTube and ChatGPT give answers. Nobody cross-questions you and tells you what was weak. (Supports H1, H2; sharpens positioning against ChatGPT.)
2. **Feedback after rejection is almost never given.** Everyone heard "we're moving ahead with other candidates". (H2)
3. **Language freezes the intro, not the knowledge.** Hindi-first candidates fail in the first 60 seconds of English. (H4, reframed: *switching* between Hindi and English is the practice need)
4. **Pay resistance is real.** Freshers won't pay; experienced people have already paid for courses and are wary. (H5; matches the survey's "free only" group)
5. **Job scams are common.** Asked to pay for registration or "guarantees". (H6; matches the survey: 6 of 14)
6. **Some users need role-fit before practice.** "Which job can I actually get?" (new, H8)
7. **Employers want proof of job reality, not polish.** Field willingness, and questioning inconsistent info. (H7)

## Product ideas worth considering (not MVP unless validated)
- Interviewer **interrupts and cross-questions** ("give me a number", "forget the dashboard").
- **Credit case file mode**: documents on screen, 15 min, then "what you missed".
- Feedback line: **"technically correct but commercially weak"**.
- **Hindi ⇄ English switch** mid-interview.
- **Role-fit check** before practice (P5), which is also a strong free hook.
- **Day-in-the-life check** for field roles ("describe your day"), which answers HR1's early-exit pain.

## 3 questions added to the real interview guide
1. "Show me the last job you applied for: where you found it, what you sent, what happened next."
2. "The last time an interview went badly, what did you do in the next 24 hours?"
3. "If HireMate didn't exist, how would you prepare for your next interview? What's missing from that?" (Ask *before* describing HireMate.)
