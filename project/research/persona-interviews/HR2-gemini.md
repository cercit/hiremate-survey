**Q1:** Tell me about the roles you hire for, and how many freshers or early-career people you interview.

Freshers and early-career folks make up the bulk of our funnel, maybe around forty to fifty people every month across these verticals. Most of them are MBAs or engineering grads who write advanced Excel on their resume, but honestly, half of them struggle with basic data formatting when we put them in front of a system.

**Q2:** Walk me through the last time you hired for one of these roles. How did you decide?
**A2:** Last month we were closing a credit underwriting position for our retail lending desk. We gave shortlisted candidates a standard case sheet with raw portfolio numbers and asked them to spot the risk flags. We ended up hiring a candidate who didn't top the written test marks, but she was the only one who actually paused and asked what data assumptions were missing before diving into calculations. 

**Q3:** Think of a candidate you rejected recently. What made them fall short?
**A3:** Just last week we had an MBA graduate who memorized every single financial definition out there and rattled off textbook ratios without blinking. But when we gave him a messy, unstructured operational case, he approved the bad loan parameters instantly just to look decisive. He lacked practical judgment and couldn't see past the clean spreadsheets in his head.

**Q4:** What happens with new hires in their first 2–3 months? Tell me about someone who left early.
**A4:** The first quarter is brutal because the actual loan processing load hits them hard, and the mismatch between what they learned in college and daily MIS reporting becomes very obvious. We had an engineer quit within forty-five days last quarter because he couldn't handle the repetitive reconciliation work and got overwhelmed by our core banking query screens. He thought it would be high-strategy advisory work right from day one.

**Q5:** How do you find candidates today, and what does it cost you in time or money?
**A5:** We rely heavily on campus placement drives, premium job portal subscriptions, and recruitment consultancy partners. Between the portal packages and the finder fees agencies charge when a candidate completes ninety days, it burns a massive hole in our annual talent budget. On top of that, my team easily spends twenty to thirty hours every week just filtering out irrelevant resumes that have copied-pasted technical skills.

**Q6:** If you could know one thing about every candidate before the interview, what would it be?
**A6:** I would want to know whether they can actually execute a task independently when given a messy, incomplete problem statement, rather than just reciting theory. Most resumes look identical, so cutting through the rehearsed interview script to see how they handle sudden ambiguity would save us hours of wasted screening.

**Q7:** If candidates came to you having already done a role-play mock with a score and a red-line check, would that matter to you? Would you pay for access to them, and how?
**A7:** Honestly, certificates and automated scores don't mean much to me because third-party apps can easily be gamed or practiced until perfected. Whether we would pay depends entirely on strict data privacy compliance and how well it matches our internal bank credit workflows, so I can't say we would sign up for it easily without a long pilot phase.
