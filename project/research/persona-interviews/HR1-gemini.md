## Transcript
**Q1:** Tell me about the roles you hire for, and how many freshers or early-career people you interview.
**A1:** Here at our vehicle-finance NBFC branch, I only hire for two frontline roles—field sales officers who sit at two-wheeler and commercial-vehicle dealerships, and collection executives who go into the field for EMI recovery. Because boys keep leaving within a few weeks, I end up interviewing freshers or one-year-experience guys almost every single month. Last month alone I spoke to around seven or eight B.Com and BBA pass-outs just to fill two seats, *aste*. Most of them have just finished college here in Karnataka and don't really know what NBFC field work looks like on the ground.

**Q2:** Walk me through the last time you hired for one of these roles. How did you decide?
**A2:** Two weeks back I hired a boy for collections after a senior executive brought him in through reference. First I checked if he had his own bike and a proper two-wheeler license, then I did a five-minute role-play right across my cabin table where I acted like a commercial-auto customer shouting that I won't pay the EMI today. He didn't lose his cool or argue back; he just calmly said in Kannada, "Anna, interest and bounce charges will pile up on your account, let's sort out at least part-payment before evening." That composure was enough for me to tell HR to issue his offer letter the next day.

**Q3:** Think of a candidate you rejected recently. What made them fall short?
**A3:** Just last Tuesday a boy came in who had worked briefly as a delivery boy and wanted to move into collections. When I played the angry defaulting customer during the interview, he immediately puffed up his chest and said, "If you don't pay by tonight, I will bring four boys to your house at 9 p.m. and lock your vehicle." The moment I heard that, I cut the interview short and sent him out—I didn't even bother explaining to him what he did wrong. Under RBI recovery rules, we strictly cannot call or visit before 8 a.m. or after 7 p.m. and we cannot threaten anyone, especially after one customer complaint came back on our branch last year and head office grilled me for a week. Another fresher the same afternoon just went totally blank and froze when I raised my voice, so I rejected both.

**Q4:** What happens with new hires in their first 2–3 months? Tell me about someone who left early.
**A4:** In the interview every single guy nods his head and says, "Yes sir, field work is fine, no problem at all." Then once they actually have to ride thirty kilometres a day in the afternoon sun chasing dealer leads or knocking on doors, they vanish before their second salary. Last month one boy I put in field sales sat at a scooter dealership for ten days, complained that standing in the heat gave him a headache, and simply stopped picking up my calls on Monday morning. We spent two weeks getting his employee code created and training him on the loan app, and *swalpa* honesty in the interview would have saved everyone's time.

**Q5:** How do you find candidates today, and what does it cost you in time or money?
**A5:** Mostly I tell my existing field boys to bring their friends or cousins, because at least someone in the branch knows their family background. When referrals dry up, our regional HR puts up listings on Apna or WorkIndia, or we use a local placement consultant here in town. I'm not sure what head office pays overall for the job-board subscriptions, but when we used the local agency in August, they charged five thousand rupees flat per joined candidate and head office paid around fifteen hundred rupees for a boosted local job post. For me the real cost is my own time: sitting through ten half-hour interviews every month in the middle of disbursement season when half the guys don't even show up.

**Q6:** If you could know one thing about every candidate before the interview, what would it be?
**A6:** Honestly, I just want to know whether the fellow will actually survive riding around in the sun for eight hours a day without quitting in fifteen days. Paper resumes all look the same for freshers, and everyone lies when you ask if they are comfortable with outdoor travel. If there was some way to know upfront that he won't run away the first time a customer shouts at him or the weather gets hot, my headache would be half over.

**Q7:** If candidates came to you having already done a role-play mock with a score and a red-line check, would that matter to you? Would you pay for access to them, and how?
**A7:** See, a score on some mobile app means very little to me—anyone can get their smarter friend to speak into the phone or mug up two lines. Maybe if I could actually hear a thirty-second recording of how he handled an angry customer in Kannada without crossing RBI timing or threat rules, it might save me calling total time-wasters to the branch, *nodona*. As for paying, from my own pocket I won't pay a single rupee, and I have never paid for any hiring tool beyond regular job-board fees. Even for company reimbursement, head office is very tight on branch expenses, so it depends—maybe if it worked like a replacement for the local consultant where we only pay a small fee after the boy stays for thirty days, I could ask my regional manager, otherwise no chance.
