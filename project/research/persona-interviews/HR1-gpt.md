## Transcript

**Q1:** Tell me about the roles you hire for, and how many freshers or early-career people you interview.

**A1:** See, mainly we recruit field sales officers and collection executives for our branch, sometimes people with one or two years' experience, but mostly freshers only are coming. Almost every month some vacancy comes because people join and leave, especially in collections. Exact number of interviews I don't maintain separately, HR will have that, but whenever there's a vacancy I'll see multiple candidates before finalising. Problem is everybody says "yes sir, field work is okay," but after joining only we come to know whether they can actually manage.

**Q2:** Walk me through the last time you hired for one of these roles. How did you decide?

**A2:** Recently we hired one collection executive through our existing staff's reference, a fellow who had previously worked in field sales for a small distributor. During interview I asked him what he'd do if a customer refuses to pay and starts shouting, and he said he'd first understand the issue, check the account and discuss a realistic payment commitment without creating a scene. I also asked about travelling to villages in summer, and he explained how he used to cover different areas on his two-wheeler, so at least he knew what daily field work means. We checked his documents and reference, then I recommended him to HR; he's joined, but it's too early to say whether he'll stay.

**Q3:** Think of a candidate you rejected recently. What made them fall short?

**A3:** One fresher came for collections, spoke confidently initially, saying he could handle any customer. I acted like an angry borrower and said, "I won't pay this month, do whatever you want," and he immediately replied, "Sir, then I'll take your vehicle, you have to pay." I asked whether he could threaten a customer like that or take a vehicle without following procedure, and he didn't know what to say. We had a recovery complaint earlier at our branch, so I won't take chances with someone who thinks collection means threatening people; I told HR he wasn't suitable, but I didn't give him a detailed explanation directly.

**Q4:** What happens with new hires in their first 2–3 months? Tell me about someone who left early.

**A4:** First we explain the product, collection process, customer handling and reporting, then they start accompanying senior field staff before going independently. One fellow joined for field sales recently, and within two weeks he came saying, "Sir, daily travelling in this heat is too much, I thought dealers would send customers to the office." But during interview I'd clearly told him dealer visits, market visits and follow-ups are part of the job, and he had said no problem. When somebody leaves like that, again we have to find a person, train him and manage the pending work, so it's a headache for the whole branch.

**Q5:** How do you find candidates today, and what does it cost you in time or money?

**A5:** Mostly staff referrals, local WhatsApp job groups, job boards and sometimes recruiters who already know our branch requirements. Job-board listings are paid through company arrangements, but I don't know the exact amount because HR handles those payments. My bigger problem is time: we call candidates, explain the work, arrange interviews, and sometimes they don't even turn up or they disappear after joining. I still have to do my regular branch business, collections review and dealer visits, so recruiting again and again becomes irritating.

**Q6:** If you could know one thing about every candidate before the interview, what would it be?

**A6:** Whether that fellow genuinely understands field work and will actually do it, not just say yes because he needs a job. Basic communication I can check in five minutes, but attitude when a customer shouts and willingness to travel daily are difficult to judge. Some candidates are very good at giving prepared answers, but when I change the situation suddenly, they become silent or start arguing. I'd like to know how they behave in that situation, especially whether they can stay calm without threatening the customer.

**Q7:** If candidates came to you having already done a role-play mock with a score and a red-line check, would that matter to you? Would you pay for access to them, and how?

**A7:** Hmm, score alone I won't believe, because who knows how that app is giving marks, and a candidate can practise the same question ten times. If I can hear how he actually handled an angry customer, whether he followed recovery rules like no threatening and no calling before 8 a.m. or after 7 p.m., then maybe it'll help me shortlist, but I'll still interview him myself. Payment depends, nodri, I haven't purchased such a hiring tool before, and branch-level spending also needs approval from HR. If they show us a few genuinely suitable candidates who actually join and stay, maybe company can consider a small fee per qualified candidate rather than monthly subscription; don't expect me to pay just to see scores.

Has not personally purchased a separate hiring assessment tool. Recruiting also costs substantial management time through candidate screening, interviewing, training and replacing employees who leave early. Might consider a small fee per qualified candidate through company approval, but not an upfront monthly subscription or payment merely to view scores.

- **What would make them try a new tool:** Evidence that candidates can handle angry customers calmly, understand recovery compliance, and genuinely accept daily field work. Audio or other verifiable examples of actual role-play performance would be more useful than a numerical score alone. Would consider employer-funded access if the tool consistently produced suitable candidates who joined and remained employed, with payment linked to candidate quality rather than access to reports.

- **Why they would not:** Does not trust standalone scores without understanding how they are generated. Believes repeated practice could make scores misleading, and insists on conducting his own interviews. Would not pay simply to access assessment results, lacks independent branch spending authority, and has not seen evidence that a mock-interview score predicts actual performance or early attrition.
