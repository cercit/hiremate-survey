Now I'm looking for a relationship manager or sales officer position in a private bank because salary growth and career opportunities are better there, I feel.

**Q2:** Walk me through the last interview you attended, from the call until the result.

**A2:** Last month I got a call from a private bank recruiter after applying through Naukri for a sales officer position. The branch sales manager asked about my dealer network, monthly targets, conversion rate and average loan ticket size, and after around 25 minutes said HR would update me. After nearly one week I followed up, and HR told me they had moved ahead with another candidate.

**Q3:** What was the hardest moment in that interview, and what did you say?

**A3:** Manager asked, "What is your average login-to-disbursement conversion rate and average ticket size for the last three months?" I knew roughly how many files I was doing, but I hadn't calculated the exact conversion, so I said, "Sir, approximately 60 to 70 percent conversion, and ticket size around one lakh for two-wheelers, higher for used cars." He immediately asked how I arrived at those numbers and whether I could show month-wise performance, and honestly I couldn't explain properly. I could see he was not satisfied, and that made me a little uncomfortable because normally I'm confident talking about my sales work.

**Q4:** After the result, what did you find out about why it went that way?

**A4:** HR told me they selected someone with banking experience, that's all. I asked whether my interview performance was an issue, and she said my profile was good but they preferred a banking background. Maybe that's true, but I keep thinking about that conversion-rate question because manager's expression changed after my answer. Nobody actually told me if my numbers were the problem, so I can't say for sure.

**Q5:** Tell me how you prepared for it, and anything you've paid for in your job search.

**A5:** I updated my Naukri profile, checked LinkedIn for similar roles, and used ChatGPT to prepare answers for "Why banking?" and "Why are you leaving NBFC?" **Q6:** If you could change one thing about how you prepare for interviews, what would it be?

**A6:** I would prepare my actual performance numbers properly instead of only practising HR questions. ChatGPT gives very polished answers, but sometimes it makes me sound like I've achieved things I haven't, and when I type my answer it says "great answer" even when it's average. I want someone to ask how I calculated my conversion or why my numbers are low, like a real sales manager. But I don't want unnecessary motivational advice, just tell me what is weak.

**A8:** After that bank interview I went back home and opened my monthly sales reports on my laptop. I tried calculating how many logins became disbursements, but some files were pending from previous months, so I wasn't sure which figures to use. I also asked ChatGPT to explain conversion rate and average ticket size, then typed a better answer for that same question. Next morning I discussed it with one senior from my office without mentioning that I had attended another company's interview.

**Q9:** If you had an interview tomorrow, how would you prepare? What's missing from that?

**A9:** Tonight I'd check my last few months' sales numbers, prepare my introduction, and read about the bank's products and sales targets. **Q10:** How do you practise out loud? Who's with you, and what do you actually say?

**A10:** Usually I type my answer in ChatGPT first, edit it to sound natural, and then speak it while sitting alone in my room. Sometimes I record myself on my phone and listen because my English becomes too fast when I'm nervous. My friend once did a mock interview with me in Hindi, but generally I don't ask others because everybody has their own work.

**Q11:** When you get rejected after a final round, who do you call first, and what do you tell them the reason was?

**A11:** I normally call my close friend who also works in financial services. Last time I told her, "They wanted banking experience, that's what HR said, but I think I messed up the numbers question also." She said banks sometimes prefer people who already know their products and systems, and I shouldn't overthink one interview. At home I just said the salary discussion didn't work out because I don't want everyone asking why I wasn't selected.

**Q12:** If an app gave you a mock interview for your role, with a manager who argues back and a report on what you got wrong, what would you think? Who should pay for it?

**A12:** See, I already use ChatGPT, so another interview app sounds more like something freshers would need. But if it actually understands vehicle finance and banking sales, asks me to justify conversion and ticket-size figures, and points out where my answer is weak, maybe I'd test it once. I'd want to see a sample before paying because many apps give generic questions only, and I already pay for Naukri. If my company was sponsoring it, fine, but personally I don't think I'd pay monthly; maybe a small one-time amount if I have an important bank interview coming up and it genuinely gives better feedback than ChatGPT.
